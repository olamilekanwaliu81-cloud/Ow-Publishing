Agents

What a literary agent really does, and how they get paid

A plain-English guide · 6 min read

A literary agent is like a trusted friend who personally knows the bakery owners, and walks your recipe straight to the right one, instead of you knocking on doors yourself.

Agents are one of the most misunderstood parts of publishing. Some authors think they're a luxury; others think they're a scam. Neither is true. Once you understand what an agent does and, crucially, how they're paid, the whole thing makes sense.

What an agent actually does

  • Reads your manuscript or book proposal and decides if it's ready and marketable.
  • Helps you polish it further before it goes anywhere.
  • Uses their personal relationships with editors to get your manuscript read, most large publishers won't even open an email from an author directly, only from a known agent.
  • Negotiates your contract: the advance amount, the royalty percentages, and which rights you keep.
  • Manages the relationship, the payments, and your future book deals.

In short, the agent is your professional advocate inside a closed room you can't otherwise enter.

How the agent gets paid

This is the single most important rule of the entire profession: an agent only earns money when you, the author, earn money.

  • Standard commission: 15% of your advance and royalties for home-country deals.
  • Foreign / translation rights or film deals: usually 20%.
  • If the agent sells nothing, the agent earns nothing, no upfront fees, ever.

Here's what that looks like in real numbers. If a publisher offers you a $10,000 advance, your agent takes $1,500 and you keep $8,500. If your book later earns $100,000 in royalties, the agent takes $15,000 and you keep $85,000. Their income is 100% tied to your success.

If anyone calling themselves an agent asks you to pay upfront, that is the biggest red flag in the entire industry.

Real agents are paid out of money they win for you. Reading fees, "submission packages", or a bill to become a client are all signs you're not talking to a real agent.
