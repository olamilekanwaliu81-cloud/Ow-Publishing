Avoiding scams

A real publisher never asks you for money

A plain-English guide · 6 min read

Most aspiring authors never finish their book. The ones who do often get exploited right after, because the same self-doubt that stops you finishing is exactly what predators are trained to smell.

This is the guide nobody hands you at the start. Learn it once, and you'll spot the trap from a mile away.

The rule that protects you

In real traditional publishing, money flows to the author, never from the author. A legitimate publisher pays for editing, design, printing and distribution, and pays you an advance on top. You never pay them a fee to be "accepted."

Hold onto that sentence. Almost every publishing scam is just a violation of it, dressed up nicely.

The rescue that isn't

Vanity presses, and the fake "hybrid publishers" that copy them, specifically target writers who feel unworthy and are desperate for validation. They position themselves as the fairy godmother who finally "believes in your book." That flattery is the hook.

The bait-and-switch, step by step

  • Friendly outreach that mimics a real publisher's interest, warm, personal, flattering.
  • A quiet shift from "we love your book" to a "contribution", "production", or "marketing" fee you're asked to pay.
  • High-pressure urgency: a limited-time offer, a slot that's "about to close."
  • A contract heavy on rights they take, light on anything they actually guarantee.
If someone "selects" your book and then asks you to pay them, you are not being published. You are being sold to.

The pre-sign checklist

Before you accept any offer or sign anything, run these checks:

  • Which way does money flow? In a real traditional deal, it flows to you. If you're paying them to be "accepted," it's not a traditional offer.
  • Who owns your rights and for how long? You should keep your copyright, and any rights grant should be limited and clear.
  • What is actually guaranteed? Vague promises of "marketing" and "bookstore placement" usually mean nothing enforceable.
  • Is the distribution real? Being "available to order" is not the same as being stocked or promoted.
  • Are you being rushed? Real offers survive you reading the contract slowly. Pressure is a tactic.

Paying for a service is not the same as being scammed

Here's the nuance that matters. It is completely legitimate to hire professionals to help you self-publish, an editor, a cover designer, a formatter, a growth manager. That's a service you knowingly buy, with your eyes open.

The scam is different: it's a paid service pretending to be a traditional publisher that chose you. The lie is in the framing, not the fee.
