Self-publishing vs traditional publishing: the two roads

A plain-English guide · 7 min read

Every author who finishes a manuscript has to choose one of two roads. The whole industry, stripped of jargon, comes down to this single fork — so let's walk both.

Think of a book like a cake. A cake needs someone to bake it, someone to sell it, and sometimes someone in between who connects the baker to the right bakery. Publishing works the same way. The question is simply: who pays to bake it, and who keeps the money when it sells?

Road 1: Self-publishing

You do everything yourself, or you hire people yourself — an editor, a cover designer, a formatter. You pay the costs upfront. But you also keep almost all of the money when the book sells, and you keep every right to your work.

This is the road of control. You decide the cover, the price, the timing, the platforms. Nobody can reject you. The trade-off is that the risk — and the upfront cost — sits with you.

Road 2: Traditional publishing

A publishing company takes on your book. They pay for the editing, design, printing, and getting it into shops. You pay them nothing. Instead, they pay you — first as an upfront sum called an advance, then as royalties once the book earns out.

This is the road of the gatekeeper. In exchange for taking the financial risk, the publisher keeps the larger share of each sale, and you give up a lot of control. You also have to be chosen first — usually through a literary agent — which can take a long time and many rejections.

Self-publishing: you take the risk, you keep more per book. Traditional publishing: they take the risk, you keep less per book — but you don't pay a thing.

The one rule that never bends

Here is the golden rule of traditional publishing, and it protects you from most of the scams in the industry:

A legitimate traditional publisher never asks the author for money to get published. If someone offers to "publish" you and then asks for a fee before accepting your book, that is not traditional publishing — it's a vanity press wearing a costume. The publisher is supposed to be the one gambling money on your book; that's the entire reason you don't pay them.

So where does a hybrid house like OW fit?

Right in the open, with nothing hidden. When you self-publish with OW, you're hiring a service — production, cover design, publishing setup, and growth — the same way you'd hire any professional. The difference from a vanity press is total transparency: you keep 100% of your rights and royalties, you own every copy, there's no warehousing markup, and you know exactly what you're paying for and why. We never pretend to have "selected" you in order to charge you.

That's the honest version of hybrid publishing: you get a professional team without giving up ownership, and you carry the upfront cost in exchange for keeping the upside.

Which road is yours?

There's no universally "better" road — only the one that fits your goals:

  • Choose self-publishing if you want control, speed, higher earnings per book, and to keep all your rights.
  • Try the traditional route if bookstore prestige and a publisher's backing matter most, and you're prepared for gatekeepers and a longer timeline with no guarantee.

Many authors even try agents first, then self-publish the same polished manuscript if the traditional door doesn't open. Nothing is wasted either way.
