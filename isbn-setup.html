What is an ISBN — and do you need one?

An ISBN (International Standard Book Number) is the unique 13-digit number that identifies your book edition. It’s how stores, libraries and distributors find and order your book, and it’s tied to a barcode on your print cover.

Here’s what trips up new authors: each format needs its own ISBN (paperback, hardcover and ebook are three separate numbers), and the ISBN records who the publisher is. Use a free platform ISBN and that platform is listed as publisher; use your own and you are.

We help you choose the right route for your goals, register it correctly, and generate and place the barcode on your cover to spec — so nothing gets rejected and your book looks properly published.

Good to know

ISBN Setup FAQs

Do I have to buy my own ISBN?

No. Platforms like Amazon KDP provide a free ISBN. The trade-off is that the platform is then listed as the publisher. If you want to appear as your own publisher, you use your own ISBN — we help you decide which fits your goals.

Do I need a separate ISBN for the ebook?

Generally yes — paperback, hardcover and ebook are separate editions and each takes its own ISBN. Some ebook platforms assign their own identifier instead; we set up what you need.

What is the barcode for?

The barcode is the scannable version of your ISBN (and sometimes price) that goes on the back of a print cover so shops and distributors can scan it. We generate and place it correctly.

Who should be the publisher of record?

It depends on your goals. Being your own publisher looks more professional and keeps control with you; a free platform ISBN is simpler and cheaper. We walk you through the trade-off.

Do I keep my rights?

Always, 100%. An ISBN identifies your edition — it never transfers ownership. Your book, rights and royalties stay entirely yours.
